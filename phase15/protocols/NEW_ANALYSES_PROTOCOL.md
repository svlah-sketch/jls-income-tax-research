## N2: proportional-schedule mechanical correspondence

**Question.** On schedules with the same proportional change in both annual bands, does reported employee taxation move in the proportion predicted by the simple frozen-base identity? This is a conditional implementation/measurement check, not a test of the legal presence of surtax: the 2023 workbook already labels the column “Porez + prirez”.

**Population and variables.** Use all main-sample units with `low24_pp/20 = high24_pp/30` within 1e−9; the review reports 494 before positive-data exclusions. Let k be that common ratio and x=log(k)−log(1+r2023). Require strictly positive employee tax and income in the relevant years; exclusions are documented without replacement. Convert 2022 HRK at 7.53450 before constructing ratios. Use reported employee tax Q and income I after contributions. The primary outcome is y24=log(Q24/Q23)−log(I24/I23), equivalently the change in the log effective withholding rate. This income normalization defines the tested version of the mechanical identity explicitly. It does not remove allowance, refund, composition or threshold effects.

**Design.** Regress y24 on x, default status, the eight original controls other than `old_surtax_pp`, and county indicators. Do not add r or any deterministic near-copy of x: the review found it removes essentially all identifying variation. The intercept/county terms absorb only common shifts; heterogeneous national relief remains a limitation.

**Three primary tests, one Holm-3 family.**

1. H_M: equivalence of the x slope to one within [0.85,1.15], using TOST. The reported composite p-value is the maximum of the two one-sided boundary tests; reject the nonequivalence null only after Holm-3. A 90% pointwise interval is also shown, but it is not itself the family-adjusted decision.
2. H_D: equality of mechanical slopes between default and explicit national-pair choices. Restrict to k=1, include default, x, default×x, the same eight controls and county indicators; test the interaction against zero, two-sided. This is a conditional difference in slopes, not a causal default effect. Main-effect and interaction coefficients are both reported. Insufficient rank remains a failed family slot.
3. H_P: the x slope for y23=log(Q23/Q22)−log(I23/I22) equals zero, with the primary design on its available positive-data sample. This is a pre-period association, not proof of parallel trends if it is not rejected.

**Reading rules.** Equivalence supports only this proportional frozen-structure approximation within the stated tolerance. Nonequivalence or failure to reject equivalence does not show incorrect administration; heterogeneous national relief and composition can change y. H_D rejection indicates different conditional correspondence by source, without identifying why. A nonzero placebo weakens a reform-specific interpretation. A coefficient near zero is not the alternative “Q2023 excludes surtax”: that alternative predicts the projection of log(k) onto residualized x, approximately 0.330 in the reviewer’s design. Calculate that projection from the exact frozen design before y is fitted, report it as a design-dependent reference and do not add a fourth hypothesis test. The earlier four unavailable actual-law slots in Table F6 remain unchanged.

**Precision and sensitivity.** Record working residual SD grid {0.02,0.03,0.04,0.05,0.10} for log-rate changes and whether the ±0.15 equivalence region is distinguishable. There is no post-result change of the equivalence region. Repeat the three tests for pension withholding, in a separate labelled Holm-3 sensitivity family, applying the same positivity/exclusion rule. Report raw tax-growth and income-growth components descriptively so fiscal drag is visible. Do not infer a 2022 unit-conversion error from faster tax than income growth; both years include surtax and unchanged nominal allowances can create fiscal drag.

## Budget weight: does the stake association correspond to money?

**Question.** Conditional on the old surtax, legal class and observed controls, do units for which PIT represents a larger budget share more often exceed the national rate? This is exploratory, with an explicit local monetary benchmark rather than a causal test of council preferences.

**Populations.** A: the 180 applicable positive-surtax decisions. B: all 301 positive-surtax units. Estimate lower and higher bands separately. Outcome is 1(applied2024 rate > national), the original upward-departure definition. Preserve A-higher separation as a failed regular-fit slot; do not delete its six large cities in the primary family.

**Budget variable.** Define b as positive 2023 net PIT-related receipts divided by total 2023 revenue (accounts 6+7) from the same PR-RAS/MFIN accounting source and organizational coverage. Do not substitute the Tax Administration resident tax total for local retained revenue. Save source accounts, consolidation status, units and signs. Units with nonpositive numerator/denominator are excluded with reasons; no offset constant is added to take logs. Compare the source numerator descriptively with resident-reported tax as a measurement check; the review’s log correlation 0.977 is prior knowledge, not a target to tune to.

**Design.** Binary logit with intercept, original band-specific stake s=100[1−t0/min(t_R,c2024)], log(b), r in percentage points, and the remaining eight pre-reform controls (including both legal-class indicators). No county fixed effects, following the existing stake models; inference clusters by county. Add r explicitly: s plus legal class does not determine r for the 129 positive-surtax units whose annual reference exceeds the ceiling. Check separation by linear programming before fitting. Use the existing working-linearization CR2 and restricted score-bootstrap routines, report their approximate nature, and check two optimizers and score/information rank for finite fits.

**Pre-fixed comparison.** The coefficient γ on log(b) is measured per unit log budget share. A local money-only derivative benchmark is γ*=0.25×s_bar, where 0.25 is a rounded coefficient already visible in the prior stake models and s_bar is the unweighted mean frozen stake in each of the four pre-fit populations. This yields a separate known target for each band/sample and is frozen in the public deposit's design manifest before estimation. It is a local approximation: if the index were linear in a multiplicative monetary gap s·b, d(index)/d log(b)=s·d(index)/ds. It is not a global structural restriction on all monetary preferences.

The design-only extraction confirms positive b for every member of A and B. The frozen targets are γ*=2.0140744681 in both A bands (N=180), and γ*=1.9909208757 in both B bands (N=301). See `budget_design_benchmarks.csv` and `design_manifest.json`; these contain no new outcome-fit result.

**Four primary tests, one Holm-4 family.** Two-sided γ=γ* in A-lower, A-higher, B-lower and B-higher. The separated A-higher slot stays p=1. Report γ=0 pointwise comparisons as descriptive secondary precision information; they do not define additional primary discoveries. Family-level simultaneous intervals may be formed with Bonferroni four-test limits and are labelled conservative. Also display whether these intervals exclude the reviewer’s fixed reference range [1.8,2.1], without choosing the most favourable point in the range.

**Reading rules.** A sufficiently precise interval below γ* rejects this specified full local monetary scaling, not all budget motives. An interval containing γ* is inconclusive unless precise enough to distinguish zero; merely obtaining a positive point estimate does not validate the benchmark. A negative coefficient contradicts the proposed monotone local scaling but may reflect endogenous budget composition. Any result is associational: both s and b are endogenous pre-reform characteristics. Changing legal class, excluding six cities or replacing b after seeing estimates is sensitivity requiring a dated amendment, not a primary substitute.

**Precision.** Before outcome estimation use working logit probabilities frozen from the earlier finite models to compute design-based MDE grids; for the separated population show nonexistence of the regular estimate. Compare MDE with each γ*, including a four-test Bonferroni planning threshold. A failed precision check is reported and does not license a wider population or post hoc benchmark.
